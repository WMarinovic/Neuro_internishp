Where to go next

Books, courses and guides for going further, from gentle introductions to specialist techniques, including how to take studies online.

These modules are a starting point. The resources below take you further, from gentle introductions to specialist techniques. Most are free; where something costs money, it is marked.

The core books

  • Building Experiments in PsychoPyPeirce, Hirst and MacAskill, Sage (paid). The most comprehensive guide, with three tiers: a Beginner section well suited to undergraduates, a Professional section on building more precise studies, and a Specialist section covering fMRI, EEG and the like.
  • Programming Illusions for EveryoneBertamini, Springer (paid). A fun route into stimulus rendering, learning PsychoPy by building visual illusions.

Official PsychoPy resources

  • Teaching and learning hubThe official, regularly updated index of courses, tutorials and materials; the source most of this list draws on.
  • Experiment recipe bookWorked tutorials building experiments from the basics upward, pitched at a range of levels and open to contributions.
  • Official YouTube playlistStep-by-step videos building experiments, from the PsychoPy team.
  • Schools resourcesA dedicated site for teaching or learning at A-level and below, useful for the gentlest possible start.

Learning the Coder (closest to these modules)

  • introPy (University of Amsterdam)Lukas Snoek’s openly available two-week Python and PsychoPy course from a Research Masters; coding-first and well structured.
  • Stimulus delivery for neuroscience and psychophysicsJonas Kristoffer Lindeløv’s openly shared PsychoPy course, strong on timing and rigour.
  • Programming for Psychology in PythonDamien Mannion’s lessons and screencasts (UNSW), a clear local-coding companion to these modules.
  • Python tutorials in SpyderReshanne Reeder’s tutorials for writing psychology experiments from scratch in the Spyder environment.
  • GestaltReVision crash course (Leuven)A notebook-based three-day course taking you from basic programming to advanced techniques.
  • Radboud University PsychoPy courseA concise, practical walk through coding experiments.

Taking studies online (Pavlovia and JATOS)

Note

Going online is a related but separate workflow. PsychoPy runs in a browser through its JavaScript sister library, PsychoJS, which is normally generated for you by the Builder rather than written from a Coder script, so the pure-Python experiments in these modules are for running locally. Once you have a browser version, you host it somewhere, and you have two main options.

  • PavloviaThe PsychoPy team’s own hosting server, which the Builder can upload to directly. It runs on a low-cost credit system.
  • JATOSJust Another Tool for Online Studies: a free, open-source server you host yourself, locally to test or on a server to collect. It has a friendly interface, links to Prolific and MTurk, and will also host experiments written directly in JavaScript and HTML.
  • PsychoPy and JATOS guideThe official step-by-step for placing a PsychoJS experiment on a JATOS server and saving its data there.
  • VESPR resource listWakefield Morys-Carter’s extensive, well-kept collection of demos and guides for getting PsychoPy studies online.
