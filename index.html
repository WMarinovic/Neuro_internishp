How to use this site

        
These modules teach you to build psychology experiments by writing Python, using PsychoPy. We deliberately avoid the drag-and-drop Builder. Once you can read and write the code, nothing about an experiment is hidden from you. You will understand exactly what happens on every screen refresh, you can version-control your work, and you can fix anything that breaks rather than waiting for a menu to offer you an option.

        
Each module builds on the one before it. Type the code in yourself rather than copying and pasting. The friction of typing is where the learning happens, and the small errors you make and fix will teach you more than any amount of reading. For that reason the code on these pages has no copy button. The finished scripts for each capstone are available to download from that module, so you can check your own version against them.


        
Working with an AI assistant

        
You will almost certainly wonder whether you could reach for an AI assistant (Gemini, ChatGPT, Claude and so on) when something breaks, and you should. Used well it is like having a patient tutor next to you. Used badly it quietly does your learning for you, and you leave the session able to run a script you could not have written and cannot now change. The difference is entirely in how you ask. Treat the assistant as something to think with, not something to think for you, and three habits keep you on the right side of that line.

        
First, you do the fixing. Ask the assistant to explain what the error means and why it happened, then make the change yourself and rerun it. Pasting its code back without reading it teaches you nothing except how to paste.

        
Second, make it explain, not just repair. A one-line "here is the fix" is worth far less than "your keyboard clock was reset before the flip, which is why every time looked too long". If the explanation does not connect to something in these modules, that is your signal to go and read that part.

        
Third, weigh the options. There is usually more than one way to fix or write something, and the assistant will happily hand you one without mentioning the others or telling you why that fix may not be the best option for your case. Ask what the alternatives are and what each costs, then decide. The decision is the part that is actually yours, and it is the part worth keeping.

        

          Watch out
          
The point is not to avoid AI, it is to stay the one who understands the experiment. Every fix should end with you able to explain what was wrong, why the change works, and what you chose not to do instead. If you cannot do that, you have collected a working line of code and missed the lesson it was carrying. The errors you make and fix still teach you the most with an assistant in the room, but only if the fixing, the understanding and the choosing stay with you.
